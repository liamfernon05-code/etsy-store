## 0. Executive summary: the 12 rules that matter most

1. Fake, concealed-incentivised or suppressed reviews are a **banned practice** under the DMCC Act 2024 (Sch 20, in force 6 Apr 2025). The CMA can fine directly, up to 10% of global turnover. REFUSE all review-fabrication tasks.
2. A business that **publishes or displays reviews** (including embedding a Google/Trustpilot widget on its own site) has a duty to take reasonable and proportionate steps against fake/misleading reviews and to present ratings that reflect all genuine reviews. CHECK any review widget or schema for cherry-picking.
3. **Incentivised reviews are lawful only if clearly disclosed, and are banned by Google, Trustpilot, Checkatrade and Yell regardless.** REFUSE to suggest any reward for reviews.
4. **Review gating** (private feedback for unhappy customers, public link for happy ones) breaches Google and Trustpilot policy and sits in the CMA's "selective / suppression" theory of harm. REFUSE.
5. The old CPRs 2008 were **revoked on 6 Apr 2025** and replaced by DMCC Part 4 Ch 1. Do not cite CPRs 2008 as live law.
6. Advertising: CAP Code s.3 (misleading), 3.7 (substantiation), testimonials rules, trust-mark rule 3.50, and for aesthetics rule 12.12 (no prescription-only-medicine advertising, so no "Botox" in marketing copy). REFUSE "best/No.1/leading" and ranking-guarantee copy without evidence.
7. A review-request message may be **direct marketing** under PECR. The ICO has not, as far as I found, said that plain review requests are always marketing. The ICO does say a "survey" that seeks public opinions is likely marketing. Default to treating review requests as marketing unless a human signs off.
8. **PECR/UK GDPR maximum fine is now GBP 17.5m or 4% of turnover** (from 5 Feb 2026). New analytics exemption exists, but standard GA4 should be treated as consent-required.
9. Regulated sectors have extra mandatory display and content rules (SRA, GDC, CQC reg 20A, FCA, Gas Safe, TrustMark, food hygiene). Encode as per-sector CHECK/REQUIRE packs.
10. A limited company's website must show **registered name, company number, registered office address and place of registration** (Companies (Trading Disclosures) Regs 2008). E-Commerce Regs 2002 reg 6 adds email address and VAT number. Good audit checks.
11. Wales and Northern Ireland make food hygiene rating display compulsory; England and Scotland do not. Regulators differ by nation (see section 7).
12. The ICO became the **Information Commission** on 30 Sep 2026 (still called "the ICO"). Update any hard-coded regulator names and fee-payee text.

## 1. Fake, incentivised and suppressed reviews

### 1.1 The law (DMCC Act 2024)

- **In force:** Parts 3 and 4 of the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 commenced **6 April 2025**. Unfair commercial practices now sit in Part 4 Chapter 1 (ss.224-252) and the "banned practices" list in **Schedule 20** (32 practices). The Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations 2008 (CPRs) were **revoked** the same day; most CPR rules were restated. Source: https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/analysis/dmcc-act-overhauls-uk-consumer-law-enforcement ; https://www.lexisnexis.com/en-gb/legal/guidance/banned-practices-under-the-digital-markets-competition-consumers-act-2024 . **Confidence: high.**
- **Review banned practice (Sch 20 para 13 and related):** it is automatically unfair, with no need for the CMA to prove effect on the average consumer, to (a) submit or commission a fake consumer review, or a review that conceals that it was incentivised; (b) offer services to produce/facilitate fake reviews; (c) as a trader who publishes or provides access to consumer reviews, **fail to take reasonable and proportionate steps** to prevent and remove fake/concealed-incentivised reviews; (d) publish review information in a misleading way (cherry-picking, suppression, inaccurate overall rating, omitting info relevant to how reviews were written, e.g. financial interest or commercial link). Sources: https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=1d4fb0c6-9bc2-4708-8975-4f61a35d06c4 ; https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/no-more-faux-five-stars-the-dmcc-act-bans-fake-reviews ; CMA guidance CMA208 https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/67eeb64fe9c76fa33048c790/CMA208_-_Fake_reviews_guidance.pdf (not readable here). **Confidence: high** on the structure, **med** on exact paragraph lettering.
- **Criminal exposure:** 29 of the 32 banned practices are also criminal offences (strict liability with due-diligence-type defences); prosecutions are by CMA, Trading Standards, DfE-NI, or Crown Office in Scotland. Source: https://brodies.com/insights/commercial-contracts-and-outsourcing/the-dmcc-act-general-updates-to-unfair-commercial-practices/ ; https://www.lexisnexis.com/en-gb/legal/guidance/banned-practices-under-the-digital-markets-competition-consumers-act-2024 . **Confidence: med.** **[LAWYER]** for any exact offence/sentence statement.
- **CMA direct enforcement (since 6 Apr 2025):** infringement notices without court; fines up to **GBP 300,000 or 10% of global annual turnover, whichever is higher** (the GBP 300k figure is for individuals per some sources; sources differ on how it is phrased); lower tiers (up to 1%/5% of turnover or GBP 30k/150k) for procedural breaches; consumer redress orders. Sources: https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/analysis/dmcc-act-overhauls-uk-consumer-law-enforcement ; https://www.shoosmiths.com/insights/articles/dmcc-new-consumer-enforcement-powers-are-now-in-force ; CMA200 direct enforcement guidance https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6808ca0d8c1316be7978e74b/CMA_200_Direct_consumer_enforcement_guidance.pdf . **Confidence: high** (10% turnover), **med** (exact GBP caps).
- **Enforcement reality as of Oct 2026:**
  - The CMA said it would support rather than enforce for the first 3 months; from July 2025 it expects compliance. It ran a review-site compliance sweep in July 2025 and updated guidance in Sept 2025 (added a guide for online review sites). Source: https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2025/09/04/avoiding-fake-and-misleading-consumer-reviews-cma-updates-guidance-102l32u . **Confidence: high.**
  - **27 Mar 2026:** first formal DMCCA fake-review investigations opened into **Autotrader, Feefo, Dignity, Just Eat, Pasta Evangelists** (suppressed 1-star reviews; staff-written reviews; undisclosed discounts for 5-star reviews; inflated star ratings). No infringement findings yet; case updates were flagged for roughly Sept 2026 (I could not confirm outcomes). Sources: https://www.gov.uk/government/news/fake-and-misleading-reviews-5-businesses-under-cma-investigation ; https://www.hsfkramer.com/notes/crt/2026-03/cma-launches-first-investigations-into-fake-and-misleading-reviews . **Confidence: high** (opening), **low** (current status).
  - First DMCCA fine (any topic): **AA Developments Ltd GBP 4.2m** (drip pricing; 15 Apr 2026). Shows the CMA does fine. Source: https://www.blakemorgan.co.uk/cma-imposes-4-2m-penalty-and-orders-refunds-in-aa-bsm-drip-pricing-enforcement-action/ . **Confidence: high.**
  - Google gave the CMA undertakings in Jan 2025 on fake reviews; Amazon in 2025. Source: https://www.lewissilkin.com/en/insights/2025/06/06/amazon-gives-undertakings-to-cma-on-fake-and-misleading-reviews-102kdie . **Confidence: med.**

### 1.2 What counts as what (for the agent's classifier)

| Concept | Definition used by CMA / commentary | Agent treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Fake review | Not a genuine consumer experience: written by the trader, staff, friends/family, paid reviewers, or bought | REFUSE to draft, post, buy, or "seed" |
| Incentivised review | Reviewer given anything of value (payment, gift, discount, entry to draw, loyalty points) in exchange | WARN at minimum; REFUSE to propose (see below) |
| Concealed incentivised review | Incentive/relationship not clearly disclosed to readers | Banned practice. REFUSE |
| Suppression | Not publishing genuine, lawful, relevant reviews (esp. negative); or moderation that blocks them | REFUSE to design workflows that drop negatives from owned/embedded review displays |
| Cherry-picking / misleading aggregation | Showing only 5-star reviews, or a rating that is not computed from all genuine reviews | CHECK embedded widgets, Review schema, "4.9 stars" copy |
| Review gating / selective solicitation | Routing unhappy customers to private feedback, or inviting only likely-happy customers | REFUSE (also platform-banned) |
| Hosting/publishing | Any trader who publishes or gives access to consumer reviews, including on its own site or via embed | Duty of reasonable and proportionate steps applies; CHECK |

Source for table: CMA208 and commentary above; CMA says a trader "may infringe if they suppress genuine negative or positive reviews, selectively promote positive or negative reviews or omit information around how reviews have been written" (https://cms.law/en/int/legal-updates/no-more-faux-five-stars-the-dmcc-act-bans-fake-reviews). **Confidence: high.**

Key point for encoding: **incentivised reviews are not illegal per se under DMCC if clearly disclosed** ("review labelled prominently as incentivised"; also the aggregate rating must not be distorted). But Google, Trustpilot, Checkatrade and Yell ban them outright (section 5), so the agent should treat "never incentivise" as the operational rule. Source: https://www.brownejacobson.com/insights/retail-law-roundup-may-2025/fake-reviews ; https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/fake-consumer-reviews.html . **Confidence: high.**

## 2. Advertising rules (ASA / CAP Code)

**Who enforces:** ASA (self-regulatory; non-broadcast CAP Code applies to a business's own website and social media, paid ads and marketing emails). Sanctions: ruling published, ad withdrawal, referral to Trading Standards/CMA, removal of paid search ads, and "Ad alerts". The ASA is not a court, but the CMA/Trading Standards use CAP-type conduct as evidence under DMCC. Source: https://www.asa.org.uk/codes-and-rulings/advertising-codes/non-broadcast-code.html . **Confidence: high.**

ASA guidance pages cite testimonials as 3.45-3.48 in some places and 3.47-3.50 in others (the latter appears in the cosmetic-interventions guidance). ## 3. Data protection and e-privacy

**Not legal advice. [LAWYER] for DPIAs, LIAs and cookie-consent design sign-off.**

### 3.1 Instruments and regulator
- UK GDPR, Data Protection Act 2018, **PECR 2003** (as amended), and the **Data (Use and Access) Act 2025** (Royal Assent 19 June 2025; key PECR changes commenced **5 Feb 2026**). Regulator: **ICO**, which became the **Information Commission (corporate body) on 30 Sep 2026** but is still branded "the ICO". Sources: https://bratby.law/data-use-and-access-act-2025-commencement/ ; https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/09/29/what-the-icos-transformation-into-the-information-commission-means-for-your-orga-102o3ni . **Confidence: high.**
- **Fines:** PECR maximum raised from GBP 500,000 to **GBP 17.5m or 4% of worldwide turnover** (UK GDPR level) from 5 Feb 2026. ICO fined Allay Claims (GBP 120k) and ZMLUK (GBP 105k) in Jan 2026 for marketing messages. Sources: https://www.policypros.co.uk/cookie-consent-changes-duaa-guide/ ; https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/media-centre/news-and-blogs/2026/01/fines-of-225-000-for-nuisance-marketing-messages/ . **Confidence: high** on the cap, med on commentary details.
- **Data protection fee:** controllers who are not exempt must pay the ICO fee: Tier 1 GBP 52, Tier 2 GBP 78, Tier 3 GBP 3,763 (unchanged since Feb 2025; payee became the Information Commission 30 Sep 2026). Some processing-only-for-marketing/PR, accounts, staff admin may be exempt. CHECK "ICO registration number / fee paid" as a hygiene item, not a website requirement. Sources: https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/data-protection-fee/data-protection-fee/ ; https://www.lolasquared.com/blog-ico-registration-fee . **Confidence: med-high.**

### 3.2 Are review requests "direct marketing"? (the ICO view)
- PECR regulates **electronic mail** direct marketing (email, SMS, WhatsApp/DMs, in-app) to individual subscribers: need consent, or the **soft opt-in** (existing customer, similar products/services, opt-out offered at collection and in every message). Source: https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guidance-on-direct-marketing-using-electronic-mail/ . **Confidence: high.**
- ICO guidance says a **genuine** survey/market research is not direct marketing, but "if a communication that purports to be a survey seeks to share the data subject's opinions, feedback or views publicly, it will likely constitute direct marketing" (per commentary on the ICO guidance), and promotional elements convert a service message into marketing. A review request whose purpose is a public rating therefore leans toward marketing. Sources: https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=a8951348-ca25-45e0-809a-25c1a62c1831 ; https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/direct-marketing-guidance/identify-direct-marketing/ ; https://www.mindmydata.co.uk/ico-assessment-rfa0857688-understanding-what-constitutes-a-genuine-customer-survey/ . **Confidence: med.**
- Some practitioner blogs claim plain review requests are "service messages". I found no ICO text confirming that and treat it as unreliable. **Confidence: low.**
- **Conservative encoding:**
  - UK-DP-01 WARN: review-request emails/SMS are treated as direct marketing. REQUIRE either (a) prior consent for e-marketing, or (b) soft-opt-in eligibility (customer bought from this business, opt-out given at point of sale and in every message) with an unsubscribe in every message; REQUIRE sender identity and a valid reply address.
  - UK-DP-02 REFUSE: do not send review requests to purchased/scraped lists or to people who never bought.
  - UK-DP-03 REFUSE: do not mix promotions into a review request ("review us and get 10% off" is also an incentive).
  - UK-DP-04 WARN: **B2B/corporate subscribers** are treated differently (PECR email rules apply to individual subscribers incl. sole traders and some partnerships; limited-company addresses are not subject to the same consent rule). TPS/CTPS checking applies to live calls. SMS/email to corporates still needs sender ID and opt-out. Source: https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/ . **Confidence: med-high.**
  - Print QR codes and in-person asks are not electronic mail: lowest-risk channels.

### 3.3 Cookies, analytics and tracking (post-DUAA)
- From **5 Feb 2026**, PECR reg 6 has new consent exceptions: **statistical purposes** (first-party-style analytics for improving the service, with clear information and a simple opt-out), **functionality/appearance**, **security**, and **software updates**; each has strict conditions. ICO final storage-and-access-technologies guidance published **29 Apr 2026**; it covers pixels, scripts, fingerprinting and link decoration, not only cookies. Sources: https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2026/current-uk-cookie-laws-insights-from-the-final-ico-guidance ; https://secureprivacy.ai/blog/uk-data-use-and-access-act-2025-pecr-cookie-consent-changes-and-how-to-comply . **Confidence: high** on existence/date, **med** on detail.
- **GA4:** several commentators conclude standard GA4 does not qualify for the statistical exception because data goes to Google, which can reuse it; the ICO says a third-party processor may qualify only if the data is used solely for the site owner's purposes and not repurposed or pooled. Marketing/ads cookies (Google Ads, Meta pixel, remarketing) still need prior opt-in consent. Sources: https://clickport.io/blog/privacy-analytics-uk ; https://seresa.io/blog/cookie-consent-tracking-data-loss/duaas-statistical-purposes-exception-doesnt-save-ga4 . **Confidence: med.** Encoding:
  - UK-DP-05 CHECK: cookie banner with equal-prominence accept/reject, no non-essential tags firing before consent (GA4, Ads, Meta, call-tracking JS). Treat GA4 as consent-required unless a lawyer approves a cookieless/server-side configuration. **[LAWYER]**
  - UK-DP-06 REQUIRE a cookie/privacy policy page listing each tag, purpose and retention.
- **Search Console and Google Business Profile Insights:** these are Google-held aggregate reports about the client's own search appearance; they do not place cookies on visitors' devices. No PECR issue arises from reading them (my inference, **confidence: med**). Personal data is limited to what the agent reads; do not export reviewer names or per-user data unnecessarily.
- **Call tracking:** dynamic number insertion sets cookies/JS in the browser, so PECR reg 6 consent rules apply unless a vendor and lawyer conclude strict necessity (vendors claim "strictly necessary"; that is contested). Call **recording** needs a UK GDPR lawful basis, a privacy notice, and notice to callers under the Telecommunications (Lawful Business Practice) Regulations 2000 reg 3. Sources: https://support.callrail.com/hc/en-us/articles/5711814948877-Dynamic-number-insertion-overview ; https://blog.swasco.co.uk/blog/uk-call-recording-law . **Confidence: med.** Encoding: UK-DP-07 WARN with a REQUIRE of a recorded-call announcement and consent-gated DNI by default.

### 3.4 Storing reviewer and competitor data
- Reviewer names and profile URLs are personal data (UK GDPR). Scraping or storing them requires a lawful basis (usually legitimate interests with a documented LIA), data minimisation, retention limits and Art 14 notice or a documented disproportionate-effort exemption; ICO treats "invisible processing" as high-risk needing a DPIA. Google Maps Terms also restrict scraping. Sources: https://www.digifeel.io/blogs/infos/google-reviews-and-gdpr ; https://www.fieldfisher.com/en/services/privacy-security-and-information/privacy-security-and-information-law-blog/data-scraping-considering-the-privacy-issues . **Confidence: med.**
- Encoding: UK-DP-08 WARN/REQUIRE: store only aggregate competitor metrics (rating, count, categories, hours) by default; do not persist reviewer names/IDs beyond the minimum needed to answer a task; retention default 90 days for raw review text; use official APIs (GBP API, Places API) rather than HTML scraping. UK-DP-09 REFUSE: do not profile individual reviewers or try to identify/de-anonymise people behind negative reviews.
- DUAA 2025 added a statutory complaints-handling regime (controllers must have a complaints process; from about June 2026) and "recognised legitimate interests". Source: https://www.michelmores.com/commercial-litigation-insight/ico-guidance-explained-the-new-data-protection-complaints-regime-from-june-2026/ . **Confidence: med.** Not encoded beyond a CHECK "privacy notice names a contact and a complaints route".

## 4. Regulated professions and sector rules

For each sector the agent should have a pack that CHECKs required website items and REFUSEs prohibited marketing. All packs: **[LAWYER]** before treating as compliance clearance.

### 4.1 Solicitors (England & Wales: SRA)
- **SRA Transparency Rules (current version applied from 11 Apr 2025):** websites of firms serving individuals/small businesses must show (a) **price and service information** for specified areas (residential conveyancing, uncontested probate, summary motoring, immigration non-asylum, employment tribunal unfair/wrongful dismissal, debt recovery up to GBP 100k, licensing applications): total cost or average/range, basis of charges, likely disbursements, whether **VAT** is included, and key stages and timescales; (b) **complaints procedure** including Legal Ombudsman and SRA routes; (c) **regulatory status**: "Authorised and regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority", the **SRA number** and the **SRA digital badge** (clickable). Sources: https://www.sra.org.uk/solicitors/standards-regulations/transparency-rules/ ; https://www.sra.org.uk/solicitors/guidance/transparency-in-price-and-service/ ; https://www.clio.com/uk/blog/sra-price-transparency/ . **Confidence: high** on structure, **med** on every item (secondary sources).
- **Publicity:** SRA Code of Conduct para 8.8 (as cited): publicity must be accurate and not misleading. Testimonials are allowed if genuine, consented and not implying guaranteed outcomes; SRA has guidance on engaging with reviews. Sources: https://inkbotdesign.com/sra-transparency-rules/ ; https://www.inherent.com/beyond-the-five-stars-a-law-firms-guide-to-compliant-client-reviews-in-2026/ . **Confidence: med.**
- **Client confidentiality in review replies:** a solicitor must not confirm client status or disclose case facts in a public reply. (Inference from the duty of confidentiality, para 6.3 in the SRA Code; **confidence: med**.)
- Encoding: UK-SOL-01 CHECK badge, SRA number, regulatory statement, complaints page, price info per service type. UK-SOL-02 REFUSE outcome guarantees ("we'll win your case"); WARN on "no win no fee" without terms. UK-SOL-03 REFUSE draft replies that reveal client details. **Scotland and NI solicitors are NOT regulated by the SRA** (see section 7).

### 4.2 Dentists (GDC, CQC)
- **GDC Standards for the Dental Team and Guidance on Advertising:** advertising must be accurate and not misleading; websites should show each dental professional's **GDC number**, professional qualification and country of qualification; no honorary/membership letters presented as qualifications; price information clear on website and in practice. Source: https://www.gdc-uk.org/standards-guidance/standards-and-guidance/gdc-guidance-for-dental-professionals/guidance-on-advertising . **Confidence: med-high.**
- **Confidentiality (Standard 4.2):** do not disclose patient information, including that someone is a patient or details of treatment/cost, in public review replies or on social media. Source: https://www.gdc-uk.org/standards-guidance/standards-and-guidance/standards-for-the-dental-team . **Confidence: high.** Encoding: UK-DEN-01 REFUSE any review reply that confirms the reviewer was a patient or mentions treatment; allowed template: "We take feedback seriously; please contact [practice] so we can look into this."
- **CQC (England only)**: **Regulation 20A** requires providers with a CQC rating to display the latest rating conspicuously at each location and on their website (homepage or an appropriate landing page), with the CQC website address and the link to the assessment. Sources: https://www.cqc.org.uk/guidance-providers/regulations/regulation-20a-requirement-display-performance ; https://aethus.co.uk/posts/displaying-cqc-ratings-on-your-healthcare-website-legal-requirements-and-best-pr . **Confidence: high.** Encoding: UK-CQC-01 CHECK rating displayed on site and links correct; REFUSE display of a rating or CQC logo for non-registered providers (ASA Action Rehab ruling, 3.50).
- Before/after dental images follow section 2 rules (genuine, representative, consented).

### 4.3 Aesthetics and medical (GMC, NMC, CQC, MHRA via CAP 12.12)
- Botulinum toxin is a **POM**; advertising it to the public is banned (CAP 12.12; Human Medicines Regulations 2012). Nurses and doctors can face regulatory sanctions as well as ASA action. Sources: https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/beauty-and-cosmetics-botulinum-toxin-products.html ; https://www.nursinginpractice.com/clinical/womens-health/nurses-must-not-advertise-botox-or-other-prescription-only-meds/ . **Confidence: high.**
- GMC cosmetic-interventions guidance: no unjustifiable claims, no trivialising risk, no promotions encouraging ill-considered decisions, face-to-face assessment before prescribing injectables. Source: https://www.gmc-uk.org/-/media/documents/guidance-for-all-doctors-who-offer-cosmetic-interventions---published-version_pdf-69113414.pdf . **Confidence: med-high.**
- **CQC scope:** cosmetic-only facial Botox/fillers are generally outside CQC regulated activities; a 2025 extension covers only high-risk procedures. **England licensing scheme** (Health and Care Act 2022) is **not yet in force** as of Oct 2026 (consultation response Aug 2025; no commencement date found). Sources: https://clinicspark.co.uk/blog/cqc-cosmetic-procedures-2025-changes/ ; https://www.policybee.co.uk/blog/a-guide-to-aesthetics-regulations-uk. **Confidence: med-low.** Encoding: do not state a licence requirement; CHECK for voluntary-register claims (Save Face, JCCP) only against the register; REFUSE under-18 targeting.
- Encoding: UK-AES-01 REFUSE "Botox/Dysport/Azzalure" in titles, H1s, alt text, schema, GBP descriptions, hashtags and ad copy; suggest "anti-wrinkle consultation". UK-AES-02 REQUIRE prescriber/practitioner registration numbers (GMC/NMC/GDC) where the client publishes practitioner bios. UK-AES-03 REFUSE before/after images not held with signed consent.

### 4.4 Financial services (FCA)
- The **financial promotions** regime: an unauthorised person must not communicate a financial promotion unless approved by an authorised firm or exempt (FSMA s.21; criminal offence); authorised firms must make communications **fair, clear and not misleading** (COBS 4.2.1R; MCOB/ICOBS equivalents). Appointed representatives promote under their principal's permission. Approving firms need FCA gateway permission (since Feb 2024). Sources: https://www.fdcapital.co.uk/section-21-financial-promotions-guide/ ; https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/cobs4 ; https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/financial-promotions-adverts . **Confidence: high.**
- Insurance intermediaries must tell customers their status and that they are on the FS Register. Source: https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/icobs4/icobs4s1 . **Confidence: med.**
- Encoding: UK-FIN-01 REFUSE to write promotional copy for regulated activities (mortgage advice, investments, credit, insurance distribution, claims management) without the client's authorisation status and FRN supplied; REQUIRE regulatory-status line and FRN in output; WARN on testimonials in financial promotions (rules are product-specific). **[LAWYER]** for any promotional copy in this sector. REFUSE "guaranteed returns/approval".

### 4.5 Trades
- **Gas Safe:** only registered businesses may use the logo; use the official artwork, with registration number; misuse is a breach of rules of registration and the brand policy allows enforcement against third parties. Gas Safe is a single register for Great Britain, Northern Ireland, Isle of Man and Guernsey. Sources: https://www.gassaferegister.co.uk/media/2472/p001_bep001-brand-enforcement-policy-v50.pdf ; https://www.gov.im/about-the-government/departments/environment-food-and-agriculture/regulation-directorate/health-and-safety-at-work-inspectorate/gas-safety/gas-safe-register/ . **Confidence: high** (brand policy), **med** (NI coverage from secondary sources).
- **TrustMark:** logo only for registered businesses, unaltered, from the portal; TrustMark notifies misuse twice and can refer to Trading Standards. Source: https://www.trustmark.org.uk/homeowner/support/logo-misuse/ . **Confidence: high.**
- **NICEIC/NAPIT/other schemes:** logo/name use needs scheme approval. Source: https://niceic.com/about-us/terms-of-use/ . **Confidence: med.**
- Encoding: UK-TRD-01 CHECK each badge/claim ("Gas Safe registered", "NICEIC approved", "TrustMark") against the client-supplied registration number and the public register; REFUSE to add a badge without proof (CAP 3.50; also DMCC Sch 20 para on displaying a trust mark without authorisation). UK-TRD-02 REQUIRE Gas Safe ID number on gas-engineer pages (best practice per Gas Safe).

### 4.6 Restaurants and food
- **Food hygiene rating:** display is **mandatory in Wales (2013) and Northern Ireland (2016)**; **not mandatory in England**, though encouraged; Scotland uses FHIS (Pass / Improvement Required), voluntary display. No legislation making England mandatory found as of Oct 2026. Sources: https://www.food.gov.uk/research/mandatory-display ; https://www.infinitasfoodsafety.com/hygiene-rating-sticker-uk-display-rules-explained-2025/ . **Confidence: med-high.** Encoding: UK-FOOD-01 CHECK that the rating shown on the website matches ratings.food.gov.uk; REFUSE overstated ratings (also DMCC misleading action).
- **Allergens:** Natasha's Law (Oct 2021) requires full ingredient lists with allergens emphasised on PPDS food; the 14 allergens must be available for non-prepacked food (verbal or written). Source: https://humanfocus.co.uk/blog/ppds-a-guide-to-allergen-labelling-rules-for-prepacked-food/ . **Confidence: med-high.** Encoding: CHECK menu pages/online ordering include an allergen notice or route to allergen info; do not make "allergen-free" claims.

### 4.7 Others (not deeply researched)
