## 3. Over-claims, folklore and misapplied surgical regulation

1. **Section 1 summary** leads with liquid-BBL material (items 2, 3, 4, 10). A coding agent skimming it will treat surgical and filler rulings as the client's rules. Reorder it: non-surgical items first.
2. **R1 and R2 import GMC and surgical logic.** R1 cites "GMC paras 52-53" and refuses sales language. R2 refuses "safe", "minimal pain" and "no downtime", which are findings about injectables. The report itself admits "documented mainly for surgery/injectables". For a device salon, CAP 1.3 trivialisation reasoning is plausible, because the ASA's cosmetic-interventions definition includes non-invasive treatments, but it is an extension. The ASA 2025 rulings also leaned on JCCP surgical-risk status. Rating should be WARN-high, not REFUSE, apart from fake urgency.
3. **Sections 2.3, 2.4, 4, 5, 7, 10** are not applicable to this client (surgeon registers, Montgomery, CQC reg 20A, GMC, Florida, FTC). Do not let rules cite them.
4. **"Mortality context" in Q3** must never be copied onto a non-surgical page. That would itself be misleading.
5. **HIS "CHECK" in 12.4** is the wrong test for this client. The correct check is the reverse: flag claims that imply medical or regulated status ("clinic-grade", "medical", "doctor", "nurse-led", "clinically proven", HIS/CQC badges) when the client has said it is a non-medical business. If a nurse or doctor does supervise, then HIS registration is likely in scope. That is a human-lawyer matter.
6. **Franchise responsibility (3.6)** is correctly inferred but rated low-med. The CAP Code binds "marketers", and the ASA treats whoever publishes or approves the ad as responsible. The franchisee is the advertiser for its local page and socials. Franchisor-supplied copy does not transfer liability. This should be stated as a rule, not hedged.
7. **Time-limited offers.** For ordinary device treatments, a genuinely time-limited discount is lawful. What is risky is a fake or rolling deadline (a DMCC/CAP availability issue) and, under the cosmetic-interventions guidance, undue pressure. The report's own caveat is right. The encoded rule is not.

**What a compliant non-surgical branch CAN say** (each efficacy statement still needs client-held evidence): describe the process (LED, cavitation, RF, vacuum, lymphatic massage), session length and typical course, how it feels, suitability and contraindications, aftercare, that it is non-surgical with no needles or fat transfer, that results vary and may be modest, that it cannot replicate surgery, what it does *not* do, price and what is included, and consent and privacy information. "Non-invasive" is descriptive and fine if true. "Safe" is acceptable only as a qualified statement backed by risk screening, not as a headline and not as "the safest". Genuine, evidenced testimonials are allowed (CAP 3.47) if the claims inside them are themselves substantiated (3.49).

## 5. Missing for this client

1. **Brand vs branch, and what "Brazilian Booty Lift" is.** The name appears to be a branded or generic UK device protocol used by many salons (several search results, plus brazilianbootylift.co.uk as a brand site). Check the franchise agreement for who writes copy, who holds evidence, and indemnities. [LAWYER]
2. **DMCC Act 2024 replaced the Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations 2008** from 6 April 2025. All misleading-action and omission points now sit there, with CMA direct enforcement (up to 10% of turnover). Trading Standards Scotland and the council enforce locally. Also consumer rights: Consumer Rights Act 2015 reasonable care and skill; the Consumer Contracts Regulations for online bookings (cancellation rights, 14-day period for services); price transparency and drip pricing.
3. **Equipment and product safety.** Electrical Equipment (Safety) Regulations 2016 and CE/UKCA documentation, EMC; PUWER 1998; risk assessment and training records under HSWA 1974; Control of Artificial Optical Radiation at Work Regulations 2010 if LED/IPL; cosmetics rules for any gels or creams. Annex XVI (EU MDR, aesthetic devices with no medical purpose, including fat-reduction ultrasound) is an EU concept. The UK position is unsettled, so ask the supplier and an adviser. [LAWYER]
4. **Insurance and qualifications.** Employers' liability insurance is compulsory if the branch has employees. Public/product liability and treatment cover are not statutory but are a de facto expectation, and insurers often require device documentation. No statutory operator qualification exists today. Say "no statutory requirement" rather than implying one.
5. **Contraindications, pregnancy, medical conditions** (pacemakers/metal, DVT/clotting, implants/fillers in area, cancer). Required by good practice and by claims-must-not-mislead logic. [CLINICIAN]
6. **Under-18s.** CAP 12.25 targeting rule applies now. Scottish Act offence and Order scope from Sept 2027 are partly unresolved (see item 4 above).
7. **Influencer and social disclosure.** CAP Code section 2 (identifiability), "#ad" placement, free treatment in exchange for posts = incentivised content.
8. **Before/after specifics.** Intimate-area images need explicit consent and care over identifying marks; same pose, lighting and interval; no filters; Meta and Google ad policies restrict before/after body images.
9. **Local SEO interplay.** The name "Brazilian Bootylift Lanarkshire" includes a location. Google Business Profile guidelines on keyword/location-stuffed names are a suspension risk, separate from legal issues.
10. **Commencement watch.** As of today (3 Oct 2026) nothing in the Act or Order is in force. The agent should state "from 6 Sept 2027" and say that commencement regulations and council application windows must be checked.
