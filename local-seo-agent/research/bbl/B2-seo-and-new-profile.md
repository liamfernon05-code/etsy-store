## 1. Executive summary (the 12 things that matter most)

1. **Name risk is the number-one issue for this client.**
   - Google says the GBP name must be the real-world name as used on signage, website and stationery.
   - Keywords and locations that are not part of that name can trigger edits, re-verification or suspension (Google's guideline as summarised by several sources, see section 4).
   - "Brand + region" is the common franchise pattern and is defensible only if the signage, Facebook and the website all show exactly that name. Pick one spelling and keep evidence.
2. **A freshly verified profile is not "safe".** Core-field edits (name, address, category) soon after verification are the documented trigger for automated re-review and suspension [vendor, med]. Make them one at a time and only with evidence.
3. **The GBP API 60-day rule is real.** Google's prerequisites require a verified profile that has been active for 60+ days, plus a website. Earliest API application for a profile verified today is roughly early December 2026 (med-high; section 2).
4. **Reviews: April 2026 changes.**
   - Google added clauses to its Maps Rating Manipulation policy on 17 April 2026: no staff review quotas, and no soliciting reviews that name a staff member.
   - Review gating, incentives and kiosks are enforced.
   - In the UK the DMCC Act also bans fake and undisclosed-incentivised reviews, with CMA fines of up to 10% of turnover (section 5).
   - New profiles get reviews filtered more aggressively (vendor).
5. **Ranking timeline: no guarantee, and I found no authoritative figure.** Vendors say 1-3 months in low-competition markets and 6-12 months in competitive ones. A Wishaw address will win Wishaw and nearby-town "near me" searches long before it wins "Glasgow" searches (section 8).
6. **Regulatory context in Scotland is changing fast.**
   - The Non-surgical Procedures and Functions of Medical Reviewers (Scotland) Act 2026 got Royal Assent on 12 May 2026.
   - Offences and the council licensing scheme start no earlier than 6 September 2027.
   - Device-based fat or cellulite treatments (cryolipolysis, HIFU, RF) appear in the lower-risk licensing tier. Whether "ultrasonic cavitation" or vacuum therapy is covered is UNVERIFIED (section 9).
7. **"BBL" is ambiguous in search and in AI answers.**
   - Regulators use "non-surgical BBL" to mean liquid or injectable BBL, which is the high-harm procedure being banned or restricted.
   - The client's device-based treatment is a different thing, so the copy must say so explicitly (section 10).
8. **Brand-level Trustpilot reviews do not equal local reviews.**
   - The brand's Trustpilot page (about 216 reviews, 4.5) appears to be dominated by reviews of the brand's practitioner training, not consumer treatments ([Trustpilot](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/www.brazilianbootylift.co.uk), search snippet; med).
   - Do not mark up the brand rating as the branch's rating.
9. **Own the GBP.** The client's business Google account (not the franchisor, not an agency, not a personal mailbox) should be the primary owner.
10. **Branch page on the brand domain.**
    - It is fine as the GBP website link, but it is a shared asset the client does not control.
    - The brand's templates can create near-duplicate content across branches.
    - Decide with the franchisor whether the client may run its own domain (section 7).
11. **Scotland-specific directories.**
    - The best-value set for a non-surgical studio is Fresha / Booksy / Treatwell, Facebook, Instagram, Yell, Bing Places and Apple Business.
    - For surgical clinics the set is HIS register, GMC, Doctify, RealSelf and WhatClinic.
12. **KPIs.** Promise activity, compliance and measured trend. Never promise rankings, review counts or revenue (section 12).

## 2. (A1) What "verified" means now, and what changes after

**Verification.**
- Video verification is now the default for many new listings. Vendors describe a 30-second to 5-minute continuous clip recorded in the Google Maps app, showing location context, permanent signage and proof of operations ([Bluehost](https://www.bluehost.com/blog/google-my-business-list-verify/), [Wiremo](https://wiremo.co/blog/google-business-profile-video-verification/), [JXT Group](https://www.jxtgroup.com/google-business-profile-verification-in-2026-new-warnings-video-requirements-how-to-stay-compliant/)). [vendor, med]
- "Accepted" means the verification was passed. It does not mean Google has endorsed the name, categories or content. Those remain subject to later automated and manual review.
- Google's Gemini models now screen profile edits before publication (April 2026, reported by vendors; [Launchcodex](https://launchcodex.com/blog/seo-geo-ai/google-business-profile-review-policy-update/)). [vendor, med-low]
- Practical meaning: the signage you filmed is the evidence base. Whatever name was on it is the name Google will cross-check later.

**What verification unlocks.**
- Public visibility of the profile.
- Review replies.
- Posts and photos.
- Performance data.
- Edits that publish.
- Eligibility for the Business Profile APIs, after the 60-day wait.
- I could not retrieve a definitive Google list of features gated on verification (low confidence on the exact list).

**GBP API.**
- Google's prerequisites page requires a verified Business Profile active for 60+ days and a website, plus an application for basic API access. Access is granted per Google Cloud project, with a stated 7-14 day review window, though people report 4 days to 6 weeks ([Google prereqs, via search](https://developers.google.com/my-business/content/prereqs); [Localith](https://localith.ai/blog/google-business-profile-api-guide/); [Xovion Labs](https://xovionlabs.com/blog/google-business-profile-api-hidden-gate/)). (med-high; the Google page itself was not readable)
- Implication for the agent: for a profile verified today (2026-10-03), plan API access no earlier than about 2026-12-02 plus review time. Until then, gather data via the dashboard, Search Console and manual exports.

**How long until it ranks.**
- No Google statement exists.
- Vendor guidance gives these figures:
  - Weeks 1-4: appearing for branded and very specific searches.
  - Months 1-3: possible pack presence in low-competition areas.
  - Months 3-6 for stable positions.
  - 6-12 months in competitive markets ([Mobile Giant](https://www.mobilegiant.net/how-to-rank-a-new-business-in-local-search/); [ClickPath](https://clickpathmarketing.com/how-long-does-google-my-business-take-to-rank/)). [vendor, low-med]
- "New business ranking dynamics": I found no authoritative evidence of a "new listing boost". Treat any such claim as anecdote.

## 3. (A2) First 30/60/90 days

### Days 0-7: lock the foundations (do not "optimise" yet)

1. **Freeze the name.**
   - Compare the GBP name with the signage, the brand page, Facebook, Instagram and any invoices.
   - If they differ in spelling (bootlift / bootylift / booty lift), do NOT edit the GBP name in a rush.
   - First decide the canonical form with the franchisor and the owner, then make signage and the GBP match.
2. **Account control.** Confirm the GBP owner is a business-controlled Google account. Add the agency and franchisor as managers, not owners. Record the recovery email and phone.
3. **Primary contact details.**
   - Choose ONE primary phone (the landline in the listings, [phone redacted], or the owner's business mobile) and one email on the business domain, or the brand's role mailbox if the franchise agreement requires it.
   - Use the same details everywhere.
   - Personal Hotmail/Gmail addresses are not a documented ranking problem (I found no evidence either way). The risk is account recovery, entity-matching at appeal and trust (section 7).
4. **Categories (UK/US names; verify in the dashboard).**

| Use case | Category names reported by vendors | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Surgical clinic | "Plastic surgeon", "Cosmetic surgeon", "Plastic surgery clinic" all exist; "Plastic surgeon" is most often recommended as primary, with "Cosmetic surgeon" and "Plastic surgery clinic" as secondary ([Direction.com](https://direction.com/local-seo-for-plastic-surgeons/), [Rocket Growth](https://www.rocketgrowthagency.com/blog/local-seo-for-cosmetic-surgery-clinics/)) | med [vendor] |
| "Cosmetic surgery clinic" | I could NOT confirm this exact name. Do not assume it exists; check the dashboard search | low |
| Non-surgical studio | "Beauty salon", "Skin care clinic", "Medical spa" are common vendor recommendations ([Resoclinx](https://resoclinx.com/blog/google-business-profile-aesthetic-clinic-checklist/)); a "Body contouring service" category was mentioned by one source and needs dashboard verification | low-med [vendor] |
| Limits | One primary plus up to nine secondary categories (vendor-reported) | med |

   - The primary category is the most influential single field. Choose the most specific accurate one.
   - Do not add "Plastic surgeon" or "Cosmetic surgeon" to a non-surgical, non-medical business. That is a misrepresentation and an ASA and suspension risk.
5. **Hours, phone, address, service-area logic.** The client has premises that customers visit, so show the address. Add service areas only if the business really travels to customers.

### Days 8-30: completeness

- **Services list.** Add real treatments with accurate descriptions in plain language. No unevidenced efficacy claims.
- **Description.** About 750 characters, factual, no offers, no promotional pricing, no "BBL" claim that implies surgical-equivalent results.
- **Photos.**
  - Exterior with signage (this matches the verification evidence).
  - Interior and equipment.
  - Team.
  - Any before/after images need documented written consent and must follow ASA before-and-after photo rules ([ASA cosmetic interventions](https://www.asa.org.uk/resource/cosmetic-interventions.html)).
  - I found NO Google GBP-specific policy on before/after images. Vendor articles say they are allowed with consent, and the Google Ads before/after restriction is a separate paid-ads policy ([Stubgroup glossary](https://stubgroup.com/glossary/before-and-after-images-policy-clickbait-ads/), [Direction.com](https://direction.com/google-business-photos-guide/)). [vendor, low-med]
  - Keep images clinical and non-sensational. Avoid nudity.
- **Booking link.** Point it to the booking engine (Fresha/Booksy/own). Use the same URL pattern consistently.
- **Attributes.** Fill only those that are true (e.g. appointment required, women-owned if applicable).
- **Posts.**
  - Maybe one per week, informational.
  - Google's content policy restricts calls to action and offers for some regulated goods and services; whether body contouring falls within it is not clear from what I could read ([GBP prohibited and restricted content, via search](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7400114?hl=en-GB)). [low]
  - Anyway, ASA/CAP rules for cosmetic interventions bar time-limited pressure promotions (section 5), so do not use "Black Friday" style posts.
- **Q&A / messaging.** Google has been reducing these; check current availability in the dashboard before relying on them (low).

### Days 31-60

- Add genuine, locally relevant photos and updates.
- Build the review process (section 5).
- Create or fix citations (section 7).
- Publish the branch's local landing content (section 7).
- Set up tracking: unique tracking parameters on the GBP website link and the booking link; Search Console on whichever domain the GBP links to.

### Days 61-90

- Review all profile data against signage and the website once more.
- Apply for API access after day 60 if needed.
- Evaluate searches: branded, "near me" and town + service.
- Decide on name or category changes ONLY if there is a documented reason, and make one change at a time.

### What NOT to do in the first 90 days

- Do not change name, address and category together. Practitioners link that pattern to suspension ([Socialpilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/reviews/blogs/google-business-profile-suspension), [GBP Guardian](https://gbpguardian.com/google-business-profile-suspended-after-edit)). [vendor, med]
- Do not add a city or service to the name that is not on the signage.
- Do not buy reviews, run review gating, or let staff hit review quotas.
- Do not create a second profile for the same premises or a "practitioner" profile for the owner.
- Do not add extra service areas to claim Glasgow or Edinburgh unless you really travel there.
- Do not use a virtual office or a shared address that is not staffed (section 6).

## 4. (A2) GBP naming rules, applied to "brazilian bootlift lanarkshire"

**What Google's guidelines say.**
- Your name should reflect your real-world name as used consistently on storefront, website and stationery.
- Not allowed in the name: marketing taglines, service or product information, location information, and keywords that are not part of the official name.
- Example guidance: "Joe's Plumbing Seattle" should be "Joe's Plumbing" unless the city is part of the registered name ([Google guidelines via search](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en); [Think Local SEO](https://thinklocalseo.co.uk/google-business-profile-guidelines/); [LocalClarity](https://www.localclarity.com/knowledge-base/does-google-have-naming-best-practices-for-businesses)). (med-high; multiple consistent summaries)
- Businesses with a storefront address should have permanent fixed signage showing the name at the address.

**Applying that to this client.**
- "Brazilian Booty Lift" is a brand name, and "Lanarkshire" is the franchise territory suffix. The Facebook page and the brand's own store page both use "Brazilian (Booty/Bootylift) Lanarkshire". That is a legitimate real-world trading style if the signage says the same.
- The risk is higher than for a typical business because:
  - the name is literally service + place;
  - the brand name contains a service keyword;
  - competitors can report it;
  - the spelling varies.
- The reading "it is a service and a place, therefore a violation" is a possible one for Google's reviewers. Whether a franchise brand with a regional suffix is treated as keyword stuffing is not something I could verify.

**What happens when such a name is accepted.**
- Sterling Sky's study of 50 keyword-in-name cases (mostly law firms, older) found Google took no action in some cases and allowed re-adding keywords up to 8 times. It sent a warning about 60% of the time, a soft suspension 20% and a hard suspension 20% ([Sterling Sky](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/50-cases-of-keyword-spam/), [LSF thread](https://localsearchforum.com/threads/gmb-keyword-stuffing-penalties-case-study-by-sterling-sky.53110/)). (med; dated, small sample)
- Sterling Sky's practical conclusion is that unless the keyword name is your legal or real-world name you face constant risk of hard suspension triggered by competitor reports, and Google cross-references the name against signage and the website ([Sterling Sky](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/keyword-stuffing-gmb-name/)). (med)
- Acceptance at verification is therefore not protection. Competitor reports and automated review can still hit it later.

**Safe path.**
1. Make the real-world name unambiguous: shop-front sign, window vinyl, door sign.
2. Make everything else match: website or branch page title, Facebook, Instagram, invoices, receipts, booking engine, email signature.
3. Keep a "name evidence pack" ready for an appeal (you get about 60 minutes to complete the appeal form, so have it ready; vendor): photos of signage, lease or business-rates bill, utility bill, HMRC or Companies House record for the trading entity, the franchise licence showing the permitted trading name, a recent invoice showing the name.
4. Ask the franchisor in writing to confirm the permitted trading name and whether they have a GBP or listing for this location. A franchisor-created duplicate is a classic cause of listing conflicts.
5. If the name is ever challenged, do not argue the keywords. Show the evidence.

## 5. (A3) Reviews for a brand-new profile

**April 2026 policy changes** (high on existence of the change; med on the detail because I read vendor/news summaries, not Google's text):
- Google published its 2025 Trust and Safety report on 16 April 2026 with new Maps protections. On 17 April it added two clauses to the Maps Rating Manipulation policy: merchants may not ask staff to solicit "a certain number of reviews", nor reviews "that include specific content, including content that identifies a staff member" ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/google-tightens-maps-review-policy-staff-names-and-quotas-now-banned/); [Linc Digital](https://lincdigital.com.au/blog/google-reviews-policy-update-what-every-business-must-know-in-2026/); [Birdeye summary](https://birdeye.com/blog/google-review-policy/)).
- Vendors say enforcement now also covers review gating (screening happy and unhappy customers), kiosks and on-premises pressure, and flags unnatural review velocity. Google already described "unusual volumes or patterns of contributions" as rating manipulation. [vendor, med]
- **Velocity-spike detection:** there is no published threshold. Vendors say a burst of reviews on a days-old profile can trigger suppression ([Ignitingbusiness](https://www.ignitingbusiness.com/blog/how-google-business-profile-reviews-are-collected-and-filtered-for-spam), [Local Owl](https://thelocalowl.com/blog/new-google-business-profile-restricted-reviews)). [vendor, low-med]
- Filtering is common even for genuine reviews. Vendors cite 20-25% of filtered reviews being legitimate, which I could not verify.
- Real reasons a genuine review disappears: reviewer account new or inactive, many reviews the same day, posted from the business's own Wi-Fi or device, templated wording.

**Compliant acquisition (practical).**
- Ask every customer, at the end of the course or by a follow-up message, with the same neutral wording, with a direct review link.
- No sentiment screening beforehand. No discounts or gifts for reviews. No staff targets. Do not ask for the staff member's name.
- No on-site device handoff.
- Spread requests naturally. Do not batch past clients into a day-one flood. Brand-new profile: aim for steady trickle.
- UK law: the DMCC Act consumer provisions in force from 6 April 2025 ban fake reviews and reviews that conceal incentives. The CMA can fine up to 10% of global turnover ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/the-dmcc-act-consumer-elements-come-into-force-from-6-april-2025), [Taylor Wessing 2026](https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/insights-and-events/insights/2026/03/the-cma-steps-up-its-fight-against-fake-reviews)). (high)
- ASA/CAP: testimonials must be genuine and not cherry-picked, and must not trivialise cosmetic procedures ([ASA](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/cosmetic-surgery.html), [Aesthetic Launch Lab](https://aestheticlaunchlab.com/blog/asa-cap-cosmetic-surgery-marketing-compliance) [vendor]).

**Replying to reviews, confidentiality.**
- UK doctors and regulated clinics: GMC guidance says criticism does not relieve you of confidentiality. Do not give an account of care. Medical defence bodies advise a standard reply that does not confirm the reviewer is a patient ([GMC](https://www.gmc-uk.org/professional-standards/the-professional-standards/confidentiality---responding-to-criticism-in-the-media); [Medical Protection](https://www.medicalprotection.org/uk/articles/dealing-with-negative-online-reviews-uk)). (high)
- US: HHS OCR has fined a dental practice $50,000 for disclosing patient information in a review reply; do not even confirm someone is a patient ([AFS Law](https://www.afslaw.com/perspectives/health-care-counsel-blog/disclosing-patient-information-responses-online-reviews), [Nixon Peabody](https://www.nixonpeabody.com/insights/alerts/2022/04/19/ocr-enforcement-action-reminds-healthcare-practices-to-avoid-phi-disclosures-when-posting-online)). (high)
- The client is a beauty / body-contouring business, not necessarily a GMC-regulated clinician. GMC rules do not bind it, but UK GDPR still applies to client data. Use the same safe reply template: thank, invite contact offline, never confirm treatments or outcomes (my reasoning, low-med; I did not search ICO guidance).
- Regulator guidance on soliciting reviews from cosmetic patients: the Royal College of Surgeons standards tell surgeons to avoid financial inducements and time-limited offers ([RCS](https://www.rcseng.ac.uk/-/media/files/rcs/library-and-publications/non-journal-publications/standards_for_cosmetic_practice.pdf), via search summary). I did not find a regulator rule specific to soliciting patient reviews. (low)

## 6. (A4) Suspension, duplicate and spam risks, and what to do if suspended

**Risks relevant to health, beauty and cosmetic categories.**
- Keyword or location stuffing in names (section 4). Google's name guidance is the same for every category.
- Virtual offices, unstaffed shared offices and mailboxes. Storefront listings must have a staffed premises and signage. Without it the address must be hidden and the profile treated as a service-area business (Google guideline summary: [Rank AI](https://rankai.ai/articles/service-area-business-google-business-profile-guide), [Local Falcon](https://www.localfalcon.com/blog/how-to-set-up-google-business-profile-for-servicearea-businesses)). (med)
- Shared addresses: Google may allow multiple listings at one address if the businesses are genuinely different, but can suspend businesses that are not distinct entities ([Sterling Sky](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/two-google-business-profiles-same-address/), [PinMeTo](https://www.pinmeto.com/blog/multiple-businesses-same-address-google-business-profile/)). (med)
- Practitioner listings: a solo practitioner should not have two profiles (individual and practice). Group-practice practitioners can have their own listing if they see patients there ([Google policy via search results](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en)). (med)
- Competitor reports and fake edits. Vendors say hard suspensions are often triggered by reports. Mitigation: evidence pack, monitoring of suggested edits, and Google's own edit-screening ([Birdeye guidelines](https://birdeye.com/blog/google-business-profile-guidelines/)). [vendor, med]
- Franchise-specific: a duplicate listing created by the franchisor or a previous owner; two profiles for the same location (one brand-run, one client-run).

**If suspended.**
1. Do not create a new profile. That is classic duplicate behaviour.
2. Identify the type: soft suspension (one listing shows "Suspended") vs hard (listing removed; account-level) ([Socialpilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/reviews/blogs/google-business-profile-suspension)).
3. Fix the cause first, then appeal once with evidence through Google's appeals tool ([Google appeal help, via search](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13597551?hl=en)).
4. Evidence must show the same name, address and phone as the profile: signage photo or video, business registration, utility bill, a real invoice ([Reviewsense](https://www.reviewsense.ai/blog/google-business-profile-suspended-appeal-guide), [Osprey Solutions](https://osprey.solutions/blog/google-business-profile-suspended-reinstatement-2026)). [vendor]
5. Vendor-reported timelines: soft suspensions 3-5 business days; hard 5-14 days; escalation 14-28 days. First-appeal success 60-75% when documented. These are vendor figures and low confidence. Never quote them to a client as promises.

## 7. (A5) Consistency work, franchise structure and branch SEO

### 7.1 NAP and the platforms

NAP means name, address, phone. NAP consistency across citations is a standard local SEO practice and a trust signal for search engines and AI assistants ([BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/learn/what-is-nap/), [Optuno](https://www.optuno.com/blog/what-is-nap-consistency)). (med; the strength of the ranking effect is vendor opinion)

Platform checklist, in priority order:
1. Google Business Profile.
2. Bing Places. It can import from GBP. Bing data feeds Copilot and, according to vendors, a large share of ChatGPT's search answers ([Search Scope](https://searchscope.com.au/local-seo/claim-business-bing-places/), [Your Web Team](https://yourwebteam.io/bing-copilot-small-business-seo-2026/)). [vendor, low-med on the ChatGPT share]
3. Apple Business. Apple Business Connect was merged into "Apple Business" in April 2026. Existing claimed locations carried over ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-om/guide/apple-business-connect/abcb205640e7/web), [PinMeTo](https://www.pinmeto.com/blog/apple-business-connect-now-apple-business/)). (high)
4. Facebook page. Category under Local Business such as Beauty Salon or Skin Care Service. Name, address, phone, hours and link identical to GBP.
5. Instagram. Name and bio consistent, link to the same booking or branch URL.
6. Booking and discovery platforms with Scotland coverage: Fresha and Booksy list body-sculpting and contouring in Glasgow and Edinburgh, and Treatwell covers body treatments across Scotland ([Fresha Glasgow](https://www.fresha.com/lp/en/tt/body-sculpting-treatments/in/gb-glasgow), [Booksy Glasgow](https://booksy.com/en-gb/s/body-contouring/1630701_glasgow), [Treatwell Scotland](https://www.treatwell.co.uk/places/treatment-body-treatments/offer-type-local/in-scotland-uk/)). (med)
7. Yell has a Lanarkshire beauty-salon category page ([Yell](https://www.yell.com/s/beauty+salons-lanarkshire.html)); also 192.com and the Scottish Directory Group. (med; value is mainly citation consistency)

### 7.2 Personal mobile / hotmail vs brand contacts

- The coordinator reports the branch uses a personal mobile and hotmail email. I found no authoritative evidence that an email domain affects ranking. Vendors recommend a domain email for citations ([Optuno](https://www.optuno.com/blog/what-is-nap-consistency)). (low)
- The real risks are:
  1. The listings I found show a landline ([phone redacted]), so the personal mobile may create two different numbers across platforms.
  2. The brand's branch page may show brand or HQ contact details instead of the branch's.
  3. A personal mailbox tied to the GBP owner account is a recovery and security risk.
  4. Appeal evidence has to match exactly.
- Fix: one primary number used everywhere, additional numbers only as secondary, role-based email on a domain the client controls, and a documented source-of-truth sheet (name spelling, address format, postcode, phone, hours, URL).
- Verify the address and town: the listing says Wishaw, but check the exact postcode and locality on signage and in Royal Mail PAF, because wrong locality text across citations is a common inconsistency (low; I did not verify the postcode).

### 7.3 Franchise or branch structure

A Companies House record exists for BRAZILIAN BOOTY LIFT LTD ([Companies House](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/10591634)); I did not verify that it is the franchisor. (med)
- **Branch page on brand domain vs own domain.**
  - Vendor consensus: one main site with unique location pages beats several competing domains. Separate franchisee sites plus separate profiles can compete with corporate location pages for the same locality ([Mak Digital](https://makdigitaldesign.com/search-engine-optimization/local-seo/local-seo-services/franchise-seo/), [Elevative](https://www.elevativedigital.com/blog/franchise-local-seo/), [Griffin Mott](https://griffinmottconsulting.com/blog/franchise-local-seo-url-strategy/)). [vendor, med]
  - But the client does not control the brand domain, so its authority, content and continuity depend on the franchisor. If the client leaves the brand, the page, and the history attached to it, go too.
  - Recommendation: use the branch page as the GBP website link now (it is real, indexed and branded). Ask the franchisor in writing about editing rights for the branch page (unique local copy, photos, FAQs, local schema). Also ask whether the client may run its own domain, and if so make it the single canonical GBP destination and have the branch page link to it. Do not run two competing pages for the same query.
- **Duplicate and near-duplicate content.** Branch pages built from one template with only the town swapped are treated by vendors as a thin-content risk ([Mak Digital](https://makdigitaldesign.com/search-engine-optimization/local-seo/local-seo-services/franchise-seo/)). Ask for, or write, unique local content: the studio, the owner, local transport and parking, real photos, local FAQs. (vendor, med)
- **Who controls the GBP.** The branch, via its own Google account. The franchisor's national profile (if any) must not claim this location. Agree this in writing.
- **Branded vs local queries.**
  - Branded ("Brazilian booty lift") queries are shared across all stockists, so the brand site and big-city stockists will often dominate.
  - Local queries ("body contouring Wishaw", "cellulite treatment Motherwell") are where the branch can win.
  - Track branded and local separately in Search Console. They behave differently.
- **Trustpilot brand vs local reviews.**
  - Trustpilot reviews attach to the brand domain. The sample I saw is about practitioner training.
  - They do not help (and may confuse) a consumer in Wishaw.
  - Local trust comes from Google, Facebook and booking-platform reviews that mention the Wishaw studio.
  - Do not copy the brand's Trustpilot rating into the branch's structured data. Google's rule against self-serving review markup for local businesses and organisations is from my own background knowledge, not verified this session.
- **Schema.** Use LocalBusiness-family markup on the branch page, ideally a more specific schema.org type (HealthAndBeautyBusiness, BeautySalon or DaySpa; from background knowledge, not verified this session) with name, address, phone, hours, geo, `sameAs` (GBP, Facebook, Instagram) and a link to the brand as parent organisation. Google documents LocalBusiness structured data as supported; FAQ rich results ended in May 2026 and are restricted to authoritative government and health sites ([SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-drops-faq-rich-results-from-search/574429/), [ALM Corp](https://almcorp.com/blog/google-faq-rich-results-no-longer-supported/)). ## 9. Scotland: regulators, registers and law (affects content and trust)

- **Healthcare Improvement Scotland (HIS)** regulates independent clinics, not CQC. Independent clinics where registered doctors, nurses, dentists or midwives provide services, including cosmetic ones, must register with HIS. Inspection reports are published and graded ([HIS independent clinics](https://www.healthcareimprovementscotland.scot/inspections-reviews-and-regulation/regulation-of-independent-healthcare/independent-healthcare-find-out-if-you-need-to-register/information-for-service-providers-independent-clinics/), [HIS choosing a clinic](https://www.healthcareimprovementscotland.scot/inspections-reviews-and-regulation/regulation-of-independent-healthcare/information-for-the-public/choosing-a-clinic/)). (high)
- **Non-surgical Procedures and Functions of Medical Reviewers (Scotland) Act 2026.**
  - Passed on 17 March 2026 (124 votes in favour) and received Royal Assent on 12 May 2026. It names non-surgical BBL as dermal-filler augmentation of the breast or buttocks.
  - The offence provision cannot start before 6 September 2027, and the council licensing scheme under the Civic Government (Scotland) Act 1982 starts the same day ([Legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/asp/2026/13/enacted), [Aesthetics Journal](https://aestheticsjournal.com/news/scottish-parliament-passes-non-surgical-procedures-bill/), [Zenoti](https://www.zenoti.com/thecheckin/scotland-non-surgical-procedures-bill-clinic-owners), [gov.scot FOI](https://www.gov.scot/publications/foi-202600526006/)). (high on dates)
  - Higher-risk procedures (injectables, ablative laser, deep peels, microneedling with RF, etc.) must be done in HIS-registered premises by healthcare professionals.
  - A lower-risk tier (licence from the council) lists cryolipolysis, HIFU, radiofrequency, light microneedling, cryotherapy and other items ([Licensing Order 2026](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/sdsi/2026/9780111064603), as summarised by search). (med)
  - **Whether ultrasonic cavitation, vacuum or "wood" therapy is covered is UNVERIFIED.** I could not read Schedule 1. The client should check the Order and plan for a North Lanarkshire Council licence if their devices fall inside it. This is a compliance and a content issue, because claims on the site must stay accurate.
- **Local enforcement already exists.** Glasgow City Council served prohibition notices in 2024 on companies and a practitioner carrying out BBL and breast augmentation, and said breaches could be reported to the Procurator Fiscal ([Glasgow City Council](https://www.glasgow.gov.uk/article/9328/Fresh-Safety-Concerns-Prompt-New-BBL-Ban), [ITV](https://www.itv.com/news/2024-05-15/botched-bbls-glasgow-bans-company-and-practitioner-after-woman-hospitalised)). (high)
- **Advertising.** ASA/CAP rules apply UK-wide, including Scotland.

## 10. (B6/B7) "BBL" vs "booty lift": search and AI-answer confusion

**What the terms mean in practice:**
- "BBL" = surgical Brazilian butt lift (fat transfer).
- "Non-surgical BBL" in UK regulatory language = liquid BBL, i.e. injected filler or biostimulator. The ASA relied on Joint Council for Cosmetic Procedures guidance treating liquid BBLs as surgical-level risk ([ASA ruling](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/nkd-medical-ltd-a25-1274785-nkd-medical-ltd.html), via search). (med-high)
- "Non-surgical BBL" or "booty lift" in the beauty trade also means device-based vacuum, cavitation, RF and similar treatments ([RHMD](https://www.rhmd.com/blog/the-difference-non-surgical-bbl-vs-surgical-bbl/), [Better Body Shop](https://thebetterbodyshopllc.com/vacuum-therapy-the-non-surgical-brazilian-buttock-lift/)). [vendor]
- "BBL" is also the name of Sciton's BroadBand Light (IPL) treatment (background knowledge; not searched). Assistants and Google may blend these.
- Consequence: a device-based studio called "Brazilian Booty Lift" competes in the same query space as the most regulated, most criticised procedure in UK cosmetic practice, and AI summaries of "non-surgical BBL" will probably be dominated by safety and regulatory framing about liquid BBL. (inference, low-med)

**Content rules that follow.**
- State plainly in the first screen of every treatment page: no surgery, no injections, what the device does, and that results are cosmetic, temporary and vary.
- Do not use before/after or "Brazilian butt lift results" language that implies surgical-equivalent volume.
- Hold evidence for every efficacy claim (ASA rule). The ASA expects marketers of all non-surgical procedures to hold evidence and not trivialise ([ASA non-surgical guidance](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/cosmetic-interventions-non-surgical-procedures.html)). (med-high)
- Whether the brand name itself could be challenged as implying a surgical-equivalent result is a question for the franchisor. I found no ASA ruling on this brand. (unverified)

**Intent map for the non-surgical client** (judgement, not measured volume):
- "non surgical BBL", "non surgical bum lift", "Brazilian booty lift", "booty lift near me": high ambiguity; local pack plus brand results.
- "cellulite treatment [town]", "body contouring near me", "body sculpting Motherwell/Hamilton/Airdrie": cleanest local intent and the most winnable.
- "cavitation / vacuum therapy / lymphatic" treatments: informational plus local.
- "price", "does it work", "reviews": need honest, evidence-led pages.

## 11. (B7) How AI assistants answer; evidence is thin

(high; US only, not BBL-specific)

- **Quality of consultation answers.**
  - A ChatGPT/Claude/Perplexity/Gemini comparison found ChatGPT "good/excellent" in 98% of cosmetic-consult prompts and Perplexity 79% ([95Projects summary](https://95projects.com/ai-search/geo-for-plastic-surgeons/)). [vendor, low; I could not trace the primary paper]
  - Earlier studies found ChatGPT gave the most complete complication lists but placed less emphasis than ASPS on seeing a qualified surgeon ([Fact or fake news, JPRAS](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1748681523005284)). (med)
- **Citations.**
  - A mental-health citation audit (not cosmetic) found ChatGPT gave no citations in 14.5% of responses, vs 7.1% for Google AI Overview and 0.3% for Perplexity ([arXiv 2609.00319](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.00319)). (med; different topic)

- **Local recommendations.**
  - BrightLocal's 2026 US consumer survey reports 58% have used AI to find a local business, with ChatGPT used by 31% ([BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/lcrs-ai-trust/)). [vendor survey, US, med]
  - Vendors say ChatGPT local answers draw on the business website, GBP, Yelp, Reddit and Bing ([SEOProfy](https://seoprofy.com/blog/ai-seo-for-local-businesses/)). [vendor, low-med]
- **Health product context.** OpenAI launched ChatGPT Health on 7 January 2026 with "not for diagnosis" framing, and made it available to all US users in July 2026 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/01/07/openai-chatgpt-health-medical-records.html), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/23/openai-makes-chatgpt-health-available-to-all-u-s-users/)). (high; US)

**Working hypotheses to test (inference, low):**
1. For BBL questions, all major assistants add safety framing and point to qualified, regulated surgeons, and will likely mention NHS, BAAPS, BAPRAS, the Royal College of Surgeons and the GMC register where they search the web.
2. For "best BBL surgeon in Glasgow" they will name large clinic groups and directory pages (Doctify, RealSelf, WhatClinic) before an unknown clinic.

4. For a client's product, build a repeatable prompt-panel (per assistant, per query, per week) rather than relying on vendor claims.

## 13. (B9) Content and schema, brief

**Surgical clinic page types (compliant):**
- Procedure pages (what it is, who it is for, who it is not for).
- Surgeon bios with GMC number and specialist-register entry.
- Risks, recovery and aftercare.
- Pricing transparency (range, what is included, deposit).
- Consent process and the two-stage consent with a cooling-off period of at least two weeks (RCS standards: [RCS PDF](https://www.rcseng.ac.uk/-/media/files/rcs/library-and-publications/non-journal-publications/standards_for_cosmetic_practice.pdf), via search; high).
- FAQs.
- Complaints and regulator information.
- Consented, ASA-compliant gallery.
- A named clinician as medical reviewer with the review date.

**Non-surgical studio page types:** treatment pages ("what the device does / does not do"), suitability and contraindications, aftercare, price list, FAQ, studio and team page with qualifications and insurance, local contact page with embedded map, and a safety page ("this is not a surgical or injectable BBL").

**Schema:**
- Google documents LocalBusiness and Organization structured data as supported. Review snippets, Article and BreadcrumbList are also supported. FAQ rich results ended in May 2026.
- `MedicalClinic`, `Physician` and `MedicalBusiness` are valid schema.org types but have no Google rich result. `MedicalProcedure`, `MedicalTherapy` and `Drug` trigger nothing in Google search today ([Halcy](https://www.halcy.ai/learn/medical-schema-markup-guide), [SchemaValidator](https://schemavalidator.org/guides/medical-schema-markup)). [vendor, med]
- Use them for machine-readability and AI parsing, not as a ranking tactic.

## 14. (B10) Realistic KPIs and what not to promise

**KPIs (planning assumptions, not benchmarks):**
- Month 0-1: profile complete; name and NAP evidence pack filed; citations live on the 6-8 core platforms; Search Console connected.
- Month 1-3: branded searches show the profile; first genuine reviews accrue (a handful per month is reasonable for a single-chair studio; target set from the client's actual customer flow, not an industry average); impressions and direction requests trending up from a low base.
- Month 3-6: visibility for town + service queries in the immediate catchment; call and booking attribution established.
- Month 6-12: a competitive pack position for "body contouring [town]" is possible but not assured.
- For a surgical clinic, expect 6-12+ months for pack presence in a competitive city, and a content-led strategy over a longer horizon (vendor ranges only).

**Do NOT promise:**
- A specific pack position, a ranking date or "top 3".
- A number of reviews, or any review "velocity".
- Reinstatement success or timing after a suspension.
- AI-assistant mentions or citations.
- Revenue or lead numbers.
- Any treatment result.

**Honest uncertainty.** Google does not publish ranking-timeline data. Google's review and edit enforcement is opaque, and vendor figures are marketing-adjacent. Law and regulation in Scotland are moving; the dates here are as found on 2026-10-03.
