Etsy rules (checked via search snippets of Etsy's own pages: etsy.com/legal/creativity, etsy.com/legal/trademarks, etsy.com/legal/ip, help.etsy.com "Reasons a Listing May Be Removed Under Etsy's Creativity Standards"; direct fetch blocked; re-read at listing time):
- Creativity Standards have four categories: Made by, Designed by, Sourced by, Handpicked by. POD items using original artwork fall under "Designed by a seller"; original designs made by seller-prompted AI and printed by a third party are allowed there.
- Secondary guides (2026) say AI-assisted listings must be marked as using AI and use the "Designed by" attribution, with a plain factual description line; and that production partner disclosure (Printify) is separate. Exact toggle wording in Shop Manager not seen: confirm.
- Etsy's trademark policy: names, phrases, slogans and logos can be marks; sellers must own or have permission; "inspired by" tagging does not shield you. A phrase printed only as decoration is generally not itself a mark, but an existing registration for apparel still creates risk.
- AI prompt bundles are prohibited (not relevant here).
- Practical: keep prompt logs, layered source files (SVG/PSD) and the font list as proof of "Designed by".

Cross-cutting production rules (apply to every file):
1. Minimum line/stroke 0.06 in (18 px at 300 DPI, about 4.3 pt). Minimum gap between shapes 0.05 in. Nothing thinner: the 50/50 fleece texture eats hairlines and the poly makes edges soft.
2. Type: headline cap height at least 0.9 in (about 90 pt fonts); label pill text at least 0.3 in cap height (about 30 pt, bold weight, uppercase); no font strokes thinner than about 0.04 in (use heavy weights).
3. Max 2 inks plus blank showing through. No gradients, glows, shadows, halftones, semi-transparent pixels (gray haze on dark garments).
4. No white on light blanks; light-ink art on dark blanks and dark-ink art on light blanks (already so).
5. Art zone: 11 in wide maximum, 0.5 in inside the print edges, about 3 in below collar. Overall art about 10 to 11 in wide even though the area can be 12 in.
6. Delivery: transparent PNG, sRGB, 300 DPI, on the stated pixel size. Keep an SVG master. Do not upscale small AI outputs; vectorize instead.
7. Back-neck tag: removed. Fine text at 2.5 in plus an extra print location cost adds cost, availability is unverified per provider, and the name is not cleared.
8. DTG on poly-blend fleece fades faster than on 100% cotton and colors look softer. Sample-wash each color group (dark heather, forest, black, maroon, sand) once at home wash instructions, photograph before and after, and note this in the listing ("soft vintage print").
9. Textured fleece: expect slight ink break at the edge of large solid areas; avoid large solid fills over 5 x 5 in when possible (cutting the fill into shapes with garment showing through helps).

## 4. Hand-cut illustration: can a solo owner produce it?

Yes, with a fixed workflow that keeps the "Designed by" claim honest:
1. Generate with the image tool as solid black on white (prompts below); generate 4 to 6 candidates.
2. Pick the best silhouette; redraw the weak areas by hand (Inkscape or Affinity or Illustrator; a free option is Inkscape Trace Bitmap, single-color).
3. Split into ink layers, recolor to the exact hexes, roughen the edge slightly with a small manual offset to keep the linocut feel (do not use blur or grain filters).
4. Typeset text in the vector editor with the OFL fonts (Bowlby One, Alfa Slab One, Fraunces, DM Sans, Fredoka verified as OFL/open in the snippets for Bowlby One, Alfa Slab One, Fraunces; DM Sans and Fredoka license not confirmed in search, check Google Fonts page).
5. Export transparent PNG at spec; keep SVG and the prompt log.
Time per design: 1 to 3 hours (table above); the husky face (#3) and brush (#2) are the hardest. If one fails, ship the other 8 first.
Copyright note: pure AI output has limited US copyright protection; hand redrawing plus the layout is the defensible part.
