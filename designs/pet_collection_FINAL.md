### Three biggest risks

1. TIME. Printify posts holiday cutoffs about mid-October (not yet posted); my estimate for an order-by date is Dec 5 to 8 (unverified). A new shop has zero reviews and no search history, samples take about a week, and Etsy needs weeks to rank a listing. Realistic Q4 sales are small; treat Wave 1 as a proof-of-concept, not a launch bet.
2. IP NOT CLEARED. No slogan has been run in USPTO or Etsy exact-phrase search. Several templates are crowded ("Yes X. No Y.", "X is a love language", supervisor/cat-review jokes). A single takedown on a new shop is costly. Do the owner checks in section 5 before listing each design.
3. PRINT AND BLANK UNCERTAINTY. Monster Digital may not offer Sand, Forest, Maroon, Dark Heather or Ash (one source listed only sport gray, Irish green, black, red, navy, white). DTG on 50/50 fleece fades and softens; dark heather is the worst case. Contrast ratios use estimated hexes. Fix: fallback blanks pre-chosen, sample and wash test before any ad spend.

## 1. Fresh-eyes review (competitive differentiation)

### What page one looks like right now (WebSearch of Etsy market pages, snippets only)

- Top results for "dog mom sweatshirt / crewneck" are dominated by: personalized items (custom pet name, pet portrait, photo, adoption date), embroidered dog mom sweatshirts, "Dog Mom Era" and "Est 2026" text lines, varsity and faded-vintage styles, gingham and floral, and Comfort Colors garment-dyed blanks. Minimalist "Fur Mama" and "Less People More Dogs" style lines also appear.
- Search context: pet-parent apparel is a fast-growing Etsy niche, gifts for birthdays, holidays and "gotcha days" are the driver, and personalization is the feature most bestsellers share.
- Gap: I found few designs that combine a full-chest spot illustration with a wry full-sentence joke in one voice. Most listings are either identity text ("Dog Mom") or custom portraits. That is our opening, but it is unproven.
- Counter-signal: our designs are not personalized and use a Gildan (not Comfort Colors) blank, both disadvantages against page one. Offset with better mockups, a "soft vintage print" honest description, and the label-pill swap (name/breed) as light personalization.

### Thumbnail test method

At thumbnail size (about 200 px wide on a phone grid) only the top headline lines and one high-contrast shape survive. I judged each design on: (1) can the first two words be read, (2) is there one bold shape, (3) is it different from the "Dog Mom" text wall, (4) would a stranger gift it.

### Cohesive shop look without a printed brand name

The look is the brand. Rules that keep the ten shirts reading as one shop:

1. Same recipe on every shirt: bold slab or heavy-serif headline (2 to 3 lines), ONE hand-cut spot illustration, one small label pill, 2 inks maximum.
2. Same type family set: Bowlby One or Alfa Slab One for slab headlines, Fraunces 900 for the soft-serif set, DM Sans Bold for labels and pills.
3. Same warm palette family: cream, chocolate brown, deep navy, charcoal, plus one warm accent per shirt.
4. Same placement: 3 in below the collar, centered, 10 to 11 in wide.
5. Same listing template: first photo is a flat-lay or hanger on the same neutral backdrop, second photo is the design close-up, third is the size chart and "soft vintage print" note, in the same order for all 10.
6. Same title formula (breed word first, plain description, no brand words).

Front print only. No back-neck tag. "Unverified" below means I could not confirm the color is on Monster Digital's Printify color list; the only list I found was sport gray, Irish green, black, red, navy, white.

Contrast numbers are from loop 2 (computed against my estimated blank hexes; re-measure on the samples). Text ink is at or above 4.5:1; accent inks are graphic fills only (3:1 target) and never carry words.
